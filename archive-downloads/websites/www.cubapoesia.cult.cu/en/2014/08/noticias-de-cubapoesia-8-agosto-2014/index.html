THERE IS TO SHOOT DOWN THE ISRAELI WALL.

By: Roger Waters

(Translation of Robert G. Krimer)

 

In 1980, a song I wrote, "Another brick in the Wall part 2" (Another brick in the wall part 2) It was banned by the South African Government, Since it was being used by the black population of South Africa to defend their right to an equal education. Apartheid imposed a cultural embargo on some songs, including mine.

 

Twenty-five years later, in the 2005, Palestinian youths participating in a festival in the territories occupied by Israel, they used the song to protest against Israel's Apartheid Wall. They sang “We need don´t no ocupation! (We don't need occupation!) We need don´t no racist wall (We don't need no racist wall!)” At that time, I didn't know what they were singing.

 

A year later, in 2006, I was contracted to carry out a concert in Tel Aviv.

A Palestinian organization, partisan movement carried out against cultural and academic boycott of Israel asked me to reconsider my decision. I had already spoken against the wall, but he was not sure whether a cultural boycott is the right path to follow. Then this organization asked me to visit the occupied Palestinian territories, to see the wall with my own eyes instead of imagine in my mind. I agreed.

 

Under the protection of the United Nations I visited Jerusalem and Bethlehem. Nothing could have prepared for what I saw that day. The wall is a horrible to contemplate building. It is guarded by young Israeli soldiers, I tried as a casual observer of another world, with scornful aggression. If it is so for me, a foreigner, a visitor, imagine what must be for the Palestinians, for the lower classes. I knew then that my conscience would not allow me to get away from that wall, the fate of the Palestinians I met, people whose lives are daily trampled in a lot of ways by the occupation of Israel. In solidarity, and somewhat helpless, I wrote that day on the wall: “We don´t need no thought control (We don't monitor our thoughts)”.

 

Realizing at that moment that my presence in a scene of Tel Aviv could legitimize oppression that was, I canceled my concert at the football stadium in Tel Aviv and moved to Neve Shalom, a farming community dedicated to the cultivation of chickpeas and also, admirable cooperation between people of different religions, where Muslim, Christians and Jews live and work together and in harmony.

 

Against all expectations, the concert became the biggest event in the short history of Israel music. 60.000 fans faced traffic jams to assist. It was extremely exciting to me and my group, and at the end of the concert I mobilized to incite young people there gathered to demand their Government to try to make peace with its neighbours and respect the civil rights of Palestinians living in Israel.

 

Unfortunately, in the intervening years, The israeli Government has not made any attempt to enforce a law that guarantees civil rights to Israeli Arabs on equality rights of the Jews of Israel.

 

The wall was built illegally by annexing more and more land in the West Bank.

I learned in Bethlehem in the 2006 some of what it means to live under occupation, prisoner behind a wall. This means that a Palestinian farmer should look at olive trees, torn root. This means that a Palestinian student cannot reach the school due to the fact that the checkpoint is closed. This means that a woman can give birth in a car, because the soldier does not let it go to the hospital which is ten minutes away. This means that a Palestinian artist cannot travel abroad to exhibit their work, or to show a movie at an international film festival.

 

For the people of Gaza, locked in a virtual prison behind the wall of the illegal blockade of Israel, This entails another injustice seriously. This means that children go to bed hungry, many of them suffer from chronic malnutrition. This means that parents may not work in a beleaguered economy, in this way they have no means to provide for their families. This means that college students with scholarships to study abroad must see as the opportunity of their lives is wriggle because they are not allowed to travel.

 

In my opinion, the abominable and draconian control exercised by Israel on Palestinians besieged in Gaza, and the Palestinians in the occupied West Bank (including East Jerusalem), along with its denial of the rights of refugees to return to their homes in Israel, It demands that people's fair mentality around the world support the Palestinians in their non-violent civil resistance.

 

When Governments refuse to act, people should be done by all peaceful means at your disposal. For some that means joining the Gaza freedom March, for others it means joining the humanitarian flotilla which attempted to bring humanitarian aid to Gaza.

 

For me, This brings me to declare my intention to submit in solidarity, not only with the Palestinian people, but also with the many of thousands of Israelis who are in disagreement with their Governments, the colonial and racist policies that this takes place, to join the boycott campaign, Divestment & sanctions (BDS) against Israel until it meets the three basic human rights in international law.

 

1. Put an end to its occupation and colonization of all Arab lands [occupied from 1967] and the dismantling of the wall

2. Recognition the fundamental rights of the arabe-palestinos citizens of Israel in full equality with the Israeli Jews.

3. Respect, protect and promote the rights of Palestinian refugees to return to their homes and properties as stipulated in the resolution 194 of the United Nations.

 

My conviction is born from the idea that all people deserve basic human rights. My position is not anti-Semitic. This is not an attack against the people of Israel. This is, However, a reason for which my colleagues in the music industry, and also artists from other disciplines, join this cultural boycott.

 

The artists were right to refuse to act in the South African city of Sun, until it fell apartheid and whites and blacks went to have the same rights. And we are right to refuse to act in Israel until the day comes – and surely will come -, that fall the wall of occupation and Palestinians live next to Israel in peace, freedom, Justice and dignity that everyone deserves.
