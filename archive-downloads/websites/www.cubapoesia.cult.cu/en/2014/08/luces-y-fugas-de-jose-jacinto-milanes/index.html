José Jacinto Milanés

Author: Madeleine Sautie.

Are there many Cubans who do not say anything these verses? Will few be who can not identify its author?

Tortola mia! Without being dam, / Made my bed and made to my table, / To a kiss now and then, / Why you've gone? What jailbreak is that, / Red feet Cimarronzuela?// Do see green leaves only you encouraged? / The cool Brook your pico invites?? / Does calls you air that whispered? / Woe is my Dove, my ground-Dove,/ That del monte has gone and there? was! (The flight of the Dove).

In effect, I can not attest to each of the responses of readers, but I'm sure that they would not be few, If not for the tell-tale title, they say without too much dust off thinking that this is José Jacinto Milanés, the great romantic poet who was born in the city of Matanzas ago 200 years.

Many special features mark the stormy life of this Bard and playwright our born a 16 August of 1814, and died in derive circumstances in 1863. It is a creator with an existence of 49 years - she much struck by madness - left on the pages of Cuban literature a Unfading mark to establish itself as one of the most towering voices of the first romanticism on the island.

These merits give faith a poetics that basically captures nature and erotic themes, rears in verses full of melancholy, Cuban identity and everyday life ?-among the most notable compositions, His soul, The kiss, Dawn and escape her... -, next to several dramatic pieces such as the count Alarcos, that was from its premiere in 1838, in the main theater of Havana, quite an event, not only the applause that brought but also the impact that succeeded in the metropolis of Madrid.

José Jacinto, the eldest son of the 14 son of a humble family headed by Don Alonso Milanés and Doña Rita Fuentes, He was born with the gift of poetry and wisdom. They say that as a child the treasure of the Spanish Theatre drank with great pleasure, Manuel José Quintana, a book given by his father, and he learned latin at the school of Ambrosio José González with his Professor Francisco Guerra Betancourt, to who supplemented teaching on several occasions. Young was the perfect mastery of the Italian and French which allowed him to translate to writers in these languages is your commitment to self taught to overcome their culture.

Its beautiful calligraphy motivated to accept it, being very young, on the desktop of a habanera hardware. However it was not in the capital, but in his hometown, to which he would return later, where he met the illustrious writer Domingo de el Monte, who engage in a close friendship until his last days.

With del Monte would attend years later Havana, invited by the renowned intellectual to spend periods in his house on several occasions. The Havana home library and the popular gatherings of who has been considered the first critical professional of the island, they were José Jacinto temporary lights that enriched his knowledge of classical and modern and gave rein to your creativity. Thus increases his literary production occurring primarily among 1836 and 1843.

For del Monte, one of the first to recognize the literary stature of José María Heredia, It was curious that Matanzas poet does not imitate the singer of Niagara - which was very common in then - novice writers and that composing with visible authenticity surrendered to his romantic texts, taciturn and tender. His works, more than the of his contemporaries, They calaron in the popular soul, for treating them exemplary teachings and criticize social injustices.

It was the scenic setting of El Conde..., first Cuban modern work, and the most shocking theatrical event of the first half of our century, that took place in the Teatro Principal habanero - and whose representation not attended Milanes never - the originator of the first of their nervous breakdowns.

Disgruntled loves would trigger in the Bard stirred distress that end up upsetting their head. The breakdown of commitment to his girlfriend and the advent of an impossible love by her cousin Elizabeth, who he loved as a child causing it in a silence which vainly tried to remedy friends and family. Hoping to calm your spirit and save his reason his brother Francisco accompanied him to the United States and Europe, without getting a visible improvement in this effort.

So many efforts to heal it ripped the Matanzas singer some rhymes, but would the poet always. Little by little it was out in a permanent silence that unleashed in declared dementia. There was no shortage at his side or friends dear or family care, but they could not rescue him of darkness where it went to his thought.

His life away an afternoon of November of 1863, in that city where often contemplated meditative River. His elbows on the bridge poem gives us forever its stamp on inquisitive and loving gesture with Matanzas, the land that saw him starting after 20 years of muteness, and that never forget the sad regret that meant mysterious farewell of his poet, He died without trial and was born poor, in the street that now bears his name.

 

 

The flight of the Dove

Tortola mia! Without being dam,
Made my bed and made to my table,
To a kiss now and then,
Why you've gone? What jailbreak is that,
Red feet Cimarronzuela?

Do see green leaves only you encouraged?
The cool Brook your pico invites??
Does calls you air that whispered?
Woe is my Dove, my ground-Dove,
Del monte has gone and there was!

Hear my plea, fear to exhale.
You why beat wing,
If you threaten to equal death
The cunning League, the burning bullet,
And the cautious jubo from the manigual?

But alas! your brain I already credited
You want to be free, Holy passion
Although I cry it I approve it
Woe is my Dove, my ground-Dove,
Del monte has gone and there was!

If you do not return, who I trust
My hidden love, My delirium,
My illusions pouring honey,
When I stare into the river,
And high Moon that shines on it?

Inconsolable, sad and wilted,
I'll dying, Maybe in my shoulder
My confidenta left me.
Woe is my Dove, my ground-Dove
Del monte has gone and there was!
