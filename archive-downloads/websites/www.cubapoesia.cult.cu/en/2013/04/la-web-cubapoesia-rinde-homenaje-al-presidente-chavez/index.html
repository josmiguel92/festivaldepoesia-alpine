FIRST PART

 

The shocking poem that Chavez wrote about the end of his days and the meeting with his mother Rosa.

Published the 5 March of 2013. Daily Republic.

 

We find in the readings of "Our Chavez", one of the most emblematic compilations of the biography of President Hugo Chávez, a shocking poetry that the National President wrote in January 1982, in the light of a lamp in his hometown Sabaneta.

(Rosa Miriam Elizalde and Luis Baez).

I am not going to betray my origins

I am not going to betray my childhood of Sabaneta-poor boy.

Immediately after that we bury the grandmother Rosa Inés, in January of 1982, I went to House of Adam and there, in the night, next to a lamp that he had in his small Studio, I wrote a poem dedicated to her.

It left me jerking. It was a kind of oath before Rosa Ines, a memory which is my Holy:

 

Perhaps someday,

My dear old,

direct my steps

to your site.

 

With the arms raised

and with joy

put in your grave

a large Crown

Green Laurel.

It would be my victory,

would be your victory,

and your people

and your story.

 

And then,

by the old mother

again the waters

of the Boconó River,

as in other times

your fields watered,

and by its banks

the cheerful singing will be heard

of your Christ was

and the soft chirping

your tile

and the clear laughter

your old Parrot.

 

And then,

in your old House

your white doves

the flight will boost.

 

And under the matapalo

bark Guardian,

and grow the almond tree

next to the Orange Grove.

 

And also plum

next the topochal

and the Mandarin alongside your pinal

and redness

the Acerola

next your Rosebush

and will straw

under your cornfield

 

And then,

the cheerful smile

of your absent face,

fill light

This hot plain

and a great ride

will suddenly be.

 

And come the Feds

with Zamora at the front,

and the catire Paez

with their courageous mil,

the guerrillas of Maisanta

with all his people.

 

Or perhaps never, my old,

get as much joy

for this place

 

And then,

only then,

at the end of my life,

I would pick you,

Pink mamma mia,

come to the Tomb

and irrigated it

with sweat and blood,

and would find comfort

in your mother's love

and you would have

of my disappointments

among the mortals

 

Then,

abrirías your arms

and I abrazarías

which time infant

and I arrullarías

with your tender song

and I'd take

for other sites

to launch a cry

that never turn off.

 

(This poem was read by the poet James D. Cockcroft, in the Cantata de Hommage to Chávez, held in Caracas, the 26 March of 2013, as the culmination of the meeting of intellectuals, Artists and social fighters of the network in defense of humanity.)

Hugo Chavez

the continent

The stars mats are enlarged

a Redeemer

Immortal protagonist

became child

discovered your design

with synchronous caress

accurate verb in the metaphor of the action

music lips
