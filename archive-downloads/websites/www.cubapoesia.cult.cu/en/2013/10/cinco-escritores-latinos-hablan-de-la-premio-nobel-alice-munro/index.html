five Latino writers speak of the Nobel alice munro

Categories:

delivery quick/fast delivery

Natalia Guerrero.

BBC World, San Juan de Puerto Rico.

(Thursday, 10 October of 2013)

Alice Munro is an author of short stories.

The opening day of the Festival of words in San Juan, Puerto Rico was just the eve of the announcement of the Nobel Prize in literature.

It was logical that the most important literary event of the “Island of charm” people will wonder who was going to receive the maximum award letters. Much speculation and even some “bet” between writers and connoisseurs.

The Tapia Theater in old San Juan sounded some candidates already natural, as the Japanese Haruki Murakami, followed by Americans Cormac McCarthy and the eternal candidate, how I say some Philip Roth.

There was who spoke about the israeli Amos Oz and even the Brazilian Rubem Fonseca. Some names were repeated on several occasions.

On the other hand, the Canadian Alice Munro's timidly rang a couple of times. Although not because it wasn't on the radar as potential worthy of the award.

In fact, on Thursday, the Festival-goers were jubilant the news that arrived from the Academy of Sweden announcing that Munro had obtained the distinction by his “Master's degree in contemporary short stories”.

Some of those who bet and speculated on Wednesday night in San Juan shared with BBC News his impressions about the Nobel of Alice Munro.

“The tale is not one minor genre”

Lidia Jorge, Portuguese writer

The Nobel Prize is impossible because there is always at least 20 writers around the world who deserve, but this was not a surprise because every year is talked of Alice Munro.

There are other names that deserve it also as the Portuguese António Lobo Antunes, Milan Kundera, It is an injustice that does not have this award, or Elena Poniatowska. But today is the day of Munro and I am very happy for her.

Munro is a writer able to make an alliance between his last staff and the global human being in an extraordinary way. It is able to speak the travel of people throughout the world.

His book the view from Castle Rock is an admirable book. Everything has around your family and about his past is extraordinary. After reading it is the idea of a magical story through the snow in which human destiny goes and comes.

Alice Munro is for me a great writer because his inner battle is a battle without large tremors. It does not make noise, speaks quiet, speaks in silence, She is the essence of literature. It is a silent Word.

The story is one major genre, is not one minor genre and this recognition, the Nobel is also rewarding poetry, It recognizes a genre that is very important today for its brevity by his poetry, by their ability to adapt to the contemporary world.

The Nobel is very masculine but in recent years there has been a tendency to recognize women as with Herta Müller and Doris Lessing, is a tendency to requilibrar things and understand the creative woman is as important as the man of today.

Our history is short, It's just that until the 20th century, we learned to write our lives, We have been closely, on the other hand the men come from far away, but in the future there will be balance.

The machismo of the literary world

Daniel Alarcón, new writer.

It is a fully deserved award. Alice Munro is a great storyteller, a narrator with great alertness and great vision. See very well. He writes prose clean and beautiful.

You have many stories that you think you know where they go but you ends surprising because you pick up another way.

It is a much-loved and very respected writer from long ago.

I do not think that there is gender in this award compensation. I think more like there is a problem of gender in literature and the problem is that men are macho and the novel “important” is very male. It is a problem that is so obvious that you don't have to talk it too.

I remember a time in Peru that I was invited to an event of literature and had only three women invited to participate. Boards put them at a table of erotic literature. That gives me a terrible embarrassment.

But the Nobel Prize is another level and nobody can discuss the literary quality of Alice Munro.

There is a story of Munro he can not remember the name, that it is so simple and natural apartemente, and in the end, it ends with a scene of a woman with a man's decapitated head. That story I was shocked.

You always have those amazing scenes.

A world of small strokes

José Ovejero, Spanish writer.

Although it sounds a little embarrassing, last night I was having dinner and I bet by Alice Munro, but I bet not only for her, but that I said that I'd like to the Nobel win is.

If they had asked me who could win had told Philip Roth with the announcement that stopped writing, that is an eternal candidate, but wanted it to be Munro.

Nobody tends to bet on a woman because the statistics are against that win a woman. The big prizes are male chauvinists in the sense that represent a lot more men than women. But on this award seems unfair to say that it is to compensate for, Alice Munro is a superb writer.

You never know why the Nobel Committee chooses a particular author, I don't know if it is a prize for the story, Although their stories are not as short, at least that I've read.

What happens is that it is fine that for once the winner is not a novelist, Although sometimes there are playwrights and poets.

I remember particularly his book too much happiness. The whole book seems a wonder.

To me left me fascinated completely, especially for the subtlety, How to build a world through small brushstrokes, of very small details, study of character, phrases that are falling almost without darte account. How wonderful.

Kafka, Chekhov, Borges... and Munro

Carlos Cortes, Costa Rican journalist and writer.

I think it is fabulous. Alice Munro I discovered some years ago. It seems to me that there has been a critical match between writers and mainly among readers that it is a fabulous storyteller.

I like as much reader story. If one part in two the tradition of the tale in the twentieth century as it would between Kafka and Borges and Chekhov, Munro. I like this line of small beings who have ordinary things and end up being extraordinary, and I think that she does so remarkably well.

The books too much happiness and hate, Friendship, Dating, Love, Marriage have extraordinary stories, they are memorable, are you in the mind.

This Nobel will is awarding a literary universe. I know that in some countries, particularly in Latin America for example, the short story, the tale, does not walk very well editorially.

There is a permanent complaint, as the tension between the libraries, the editors, the writers because the story does not circulate very well.

But in the English-speaking world that is completely different. In Europe there are also countries where is much read story.

It seems to me that most gender award has to do with the way in Munro builds a world of privacy, populated by small stories woven using very few elements.

It is admirable as a good writer does not need to tell an epic story to tell the world and get inside it. They are poignant tales.

An award for women writers

Santiago Gamboa, Colombian writer.

It seems to me that it is a prize for the genre of the short story, but it is also good that there are writers.

The same candidates are male, If you look for every woman there 10 men. But it is also that this is a world built with gender equality.

In each country, by each writer, there are many more men than women.

Once in Paris invited writers 12 Colombians and only invited a woman, It could not go.

But it was not a gender thing is that were the writers that were translated into French. In this regard would have to talk to the editors.

If one had to choose five Colombian writers for example, There is no woman who has a work like the Alice Munro.
