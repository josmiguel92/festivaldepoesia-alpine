ROQUE DALTON. A BRIEF ANTHOLOGY
(Prepared for this bulletin by Renato Sandoval Bacigalupo (Peru, 1957)

Poet, essayist, lawyer and Salvadoran anthropologist born in the city of San Salvador in 1935.
He was initially educated by the Jesuits and later studied at universities in your country, Mexico and Chile. Played in the Communist Party from a young age, dedicating himself to literature, poetry and politics. He earned three times the Central American poetry award, the Casa de las Américas Prize and other awards in various national and Central American competitions.
His extensive poetic work, initiated in 1956, they deserve to stand out: Mine next to the birds (1957), The window in the face (1961), The sea (1962), The shift of the offended (1963), The testimonies (1964), Poems (1968),
Tavern and other places (1969) and small Hells (1970).
He was killed in 1975.

Roque Dalton was born in El Salvador in 1935. He lived as a political exile in Guatemala, Mexico, Czechoslovakia and Cuba. In his own country he was hunted and imprisoned many times. As an internationally prize-winning poet, I have also published books on literature, social science and political theory. He returned to El Salvador to join a revolutionary army. As part of a misdirected internal struggle later repudiated by all parties, Roque Dalton was killed in 1975.

THE WARRIOR'S RESTING PLACE
The dead are getting more restless each day.
They used to be easy
we would put on stiff collars flowers
praised their names on long lists
shrines of the homeland
remarkable shadows
monstrous marble.
The corpses signed away for posterity
returned to formation

they're becoming the majority!
